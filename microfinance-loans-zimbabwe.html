Microfinance Loans in Zimbabwe: How to Check Your Lender Is Registered

How microfinance loans in Zimbabwe work, how to check a lender is registered with the RBZ, and the warning signs of an unregistered money lender.

Apply Now Talk to a consultant

By Kredibility Finance · Published 22 October 2026

Microfinance loans in Zimbabwe help individuals and small businesses get credit quickly, often without the paperwork a bank needs. But not every lender is regulated. This guide explains how microfinance works, how to check a lender is registered, and the warning signs of an informal money lender.

What is a microfinance institution?

A microfinance institution (MFI) provides smaller loans to individuals and small businesses. In Zimbabwe, MFIs are registered and supervised by the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe under the Microfinance Act. A credit-only MFI lends money but does not take deposits.

Kredibility Finance (Private) Limited is a credit-only microfinance institution registered with, and supervised by, the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe under the Microfinance Act. You can read more on our governance and regulation page.

Why borrowing from a registered lender matters

  • Written terms: you receive a signed agreement and a loan schedule that shows everything payable.
  • Clear pricing: the interest method and fees are disclosed before you sign.
  • Cooling-off rights: at Kredibility, you have three working days after the loan is paid out.
  • Proper complaints routes: a regulated lender is accountable to its regulator.
  • Protection of your information: your personal data is handled under a published privacy policy.

How to check a lender is registered

  1. Ask the lender for its full registered company name and its registration as a microfinance institution.
  2. Check that the same name appears on its website, agreements and receipts.
  3. If in doubt, contact the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe to confirm the institution's status.
  4. Make sure the lender has a physical office address you can visit.
  5. Read the written terms before signing anything.

Warning signs of an unregistered money lender

  • No written agreement, or pressure to sign without reading.
  • Asking you to hand over your bank card, PIN or national ID as security.
  • Charges that are only explained verbally, or that change after you borrow.
  • No office address, only a phone number or social media page.
  • Threats or public shaming when a payment is late.

If a lender shows any of these signs, walk away.

Types of microfinance loans in Zimbabwe

Most MFIs offer salary-based loans, business loans and short-term loans for specific needs. Kredibility's range includes civil servant payroll loans, private sector employee loans, SME business loans, school fees loans, emergency loans, ZiG loans and asset-backed loans. What a registered lender will ask from you

Expect to provide your national ID, proof of income and bank details, and to go through an affordability assessment. A responsible lender only approves an amount you can repay comfortably, so being asked questions about your income and existing debts is a good sign, not a bad one.

Frequently asked questions

Is Kredibility Finance registered with the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe?

Yes. Kredibility Finance is a credit-only microfinance institution registered with, and supervised by, the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe under the Microfinance Act.

What is the difference between a microfinance institution and a bank?

Microfinance institutions focus on smaller loans for individuals and small businesses, and a credit-only MFI does not take deposits. Banks offer a wider range of services and usually need more paperwork.

How can I tell if a money lender is illegal?

Warning signs include no written agreement, asking for your bank card or PIN as security, charges that are only explained verbally, no physical office and threatening behaviour. Ask for registration details and check them before borrowing.
