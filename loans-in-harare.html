Loans in Harare: A Guide to Every Type of Loan and Who It Is For

A plain-English guide to loans in Harare: payroll, salary, business, school fees, emergency, ZiG and asset-backed loans, and who each one suits.

Apply Now Talk to a consultant

By Kredibility Finance · Published 22 October 2026

Looking for loans in Harare can be confusing. Banks, microfinance institutions, mobile lenders and informal money lenders all advertise credit, and the terms vary widely. This guide explains the main types of loan available in Harare, who each one suits, and what to check before you borrow.

Kredibility Finance (Private) Limited is a credit-only microfinance institution registered with, and supervised by, the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe under the Microfinance Act. Every facility is assessed on verifiable income and affordability, and the full terms are shown to you before you sign.

Which loan is right for you?

  • You work for the government: a payroll loan is usually the simplest option, because repayments come straight from your salary. Read our guide to loans for civil servants in Zimbabwe.
  • You work for a private company: a salary-based loan built on employer confirmation and your payslip. See salary-based loans in Harare.
  • You run a business: an SME loan assessed on your bank statements and trading records, not a payslip. See business loans without collateral.
  • You need money for a specific bill: school fees and emergency loans are designed for short-term, time-sensitive needs. See school fees loans in Zimbabwe and emergency loans in Harare.
  • You own a car or equipment: an asset-backed loan can help where income documents are limited. See asset-backed loans in Harare.

Microfinance institution, bank or money lender?

Banks tend to need more paperwork and take longer. Registered microfinance institutions focus on smaller, faster loans for individuals and small businesses, and must follow Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe rules. Informal money lenders may be quick, but they often have no written agreement and unclear charges.

Before borrowing from anyone, ask for their registration and read the written terms. Our guide on how to check a microfinance lender is registered explains what to look for.

What every lender should tell you before you sign

  • The interest rate and how it is calculated. At Kredibility, interest is a fixed rate on a reducing balance, confirmed in your client loan schedule.
  • Any fees, and whether they are deducted from the loan before you receive it.
  • The total amount you will repay and the monthly instalment.
  • What happens if you miss a payment or want to repay early.
  • Your cooling-off rights. Kredibility allows three working days after the loan is paid out.

For current rates, contact the credit team. A consultant discusses pricing, repayment terms and early settlement with you when your facility is structured.

Frequently asked questions

What types of loans can I get in Harare?

Common options include payroll loans for civil servants, private sector salary loans, SME business loans, school fees and emergency loans, ZiG loans and asset-backed loans. Each is assessed on your income and ability to repay.

How quickly can I get a loan in Harare?

At Kredibility, payroll-backed applications are typically decided within 24 hours of receiving complete documents, private sector loans within 24 to 48 hours, and SME and asset-backed loans within 48 to 72 hours.

Is Kredibility Finance a registered lender?

Yes. Kredibility Finance is a credit-only microfinance institution registered with the Reserve Bank of Zimbabwe under the Microfinance Act.

What are the interest rates on loans in Harare?

Rates depend on the lender, the loan type and your profile. Kredibility charges a fixed rate on a reducing balance, and a consultant confirms current rates before any facility is finalised.
