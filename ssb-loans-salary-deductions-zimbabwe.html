SSB Loans in Zimbabwe: How Salary Deductions Work for Civil Servants

How SSB loans work for civil servants in Zimbabwe: stop order deductions, the 50% limit, what to check on your payslip and who to contact about a deduction.

Apply Now Ask on WhatsApp

By Kredibility Finance · Published 25 November 2026

An SSB loan is a loan for a government employee that is repaid straight from the salary paid by the Salary Service Bureau (SSB), the government department that pays civil servants. This guide explains how SSB deductions work, the limit on how much can be deducted, and what to do if a deduction on your payslip looks wrong.

How an SSB deduction works

  1. You sign a stop order. When your loan is approved, you sign a stop order form that allows an agreed amount to be deducted from your salary each month.
  2. The deduction is set up on the payroll. The stop order is registered so the instalment is taken through the SSB payroll.
  3. SSB deducts and pays the lender. Each month the instalment shows on your payslip and is sent on to the lender until the loan is repaid.

Lenders should only process a deduction where you have signed a stop order, and they must keep the signed form. Never sign a blank form or one for an amount you have not agreed.

The 50% limit

Lenders are required to keep loan repayments to no more than 50% of your net monthly salary. This protects you from being left with too little to live on. Before you borrow, add up every deduction already on your payslip, including other loans, medical aid and union subscriptions, and check what is left.

Interest also has a ceiling: under the in duplum rule in Zimbabwean law, unpaid interest cannot grow beyond the amount still owed on the loan. Ask any lender for a written schedule showing every instalment and the total you will repay.

How to check your deductions

  • Read every payslip. Each stop order shows as a separate line. Make sure you recognise every one.
  • Match it to your loan schedule. The amount and number of months should agree with what you signed.
  • Check the end date. When the loan is repaid, the deduction should stop. If it does not, contact the lender and your HR office.

Who to contact about an SSB deduction

  • Your ministry's HR or pay office is the first stop for anything on your payslip.
  • The lender can give you a loan statement showing what has been received and what is still owed.
  • The Public Service Commission publishes its official contact details on the Government of Zimbabwe portal: 6th Floor, Social Security Centre, Julius Nyerere Way/Sam Nujoma Street, Harare.

Kredibility cannot change SSB records. If you have a Kredibility loan, contact us and ask for your loan statement.

Frequently asked questions

What is an SSB loan?

An SSB loan is a loan for a government employee that is repaid through a monthly deduction from the salary paid by the Salary Service Bureau (SSB). You sign a stop order, and the agreed instalment is taken from your pay and sent to the lender.

How much of my salary can go to loan repayments?

Lenders are required to keep loan repayments to no more than 50% of your net monthly salary. Add up every deduction already on your payslip before you take on a new loan.

Does Kredibility offer SSB loans?

Yes. Kredibility is an RBZ-licensed microfinance institution. SSB loans carry 10% interest, a 5% admin fee deducted on payout and a term of 1 to 6 months, with no application fee.

What do I need to apply for an SSB loan?

Your national ID, a passport-size photo, a current payslip, your EC number and your bank account details.

Who do I contact about a deduction on my payslip?

Start with the HR or pay office at your ministry. For questions about the lender, contact the lender directly and ask for your loan statement. The Public Service Commission's official contact details are listed on the Government of Zimbabwe portal.

Can I cancel an SSB deduction?

Not on your own while the loan is still running. The deduction stops once the loan is repaid in full. If you think a deduction is wrong, raise it with your HR office and the lender in writing.
